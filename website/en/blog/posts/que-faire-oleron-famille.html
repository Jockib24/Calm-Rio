What to Do on Oléron as a Family?

10 must-do activities for a successful vacation

FamilyJune 1, 20267 min read

What to Do on Oléron as a Family? 10 Must-Do Activities

Île d'Oléron, nicknamed the "Luminous Island," is a true paradise for families. Between its wild beaches, preserved forests, authentic villages, and renowned gastronomy, there's something for everyone. Here's our top 10 activities not to miss during your stay on the largest island of the French Atlantic coast.

Nature & Discovery

1. Chassiron Lighthouse — 360° Views

An emblem of Île d'Oléron, the Chassiron lighthouse rises 46 meters above the ocean. After climbing its 224 steps, you'll be rewarded with breathtaking panoramic views of the island, the ocean, and the oyster farms. The landscaped garden at the foot of the lighthouse, shaped like a compass rose, is a pleasant walk for the whole family. Children will love the small museum tracing the history of the lighthouse and navigation.

Good to know: open every day in summer. Plan to arrive in the morning to avoid the crowds and the heat in the stairwell.

2. Saint-Trojan State Forest — On Foot or by Bike

This immense 2,000-hectare forest is a unique green gem: a coastal forest planted on dunes in the 19th century to stabilize the sand. Today, it's a paradise for walks and bike rides. Marked trails of all levels cross through it, offering varied atmospheres between maritime pines, holm oaks, and flowering undergrowth. The scent of pines, the song of cicadas, and the light filtering through the trees create a magical atmosphere.

CalmRio tip: from our villa and house in Saint-Trojan-les-Bains, the forest is directly accessible by bike via cycle paths. Perfect for an impromptu outing!

3. Birdwatching — A Natural Spectacle

Île d'Oléron is a premier ornithology destination in France. The salt marshes, mudflats, and dunes are home to exceptional birdlife: grey herons, little egrets, pied avocets, and many other species. The best place to observe them is the Moëze-Oléron Nature Reserve, located on the mainland but just a few minutes from the bridge. Guided tours are organized in summer, ideal for sparking children's curiosity.

Beaches & Swimming

4. Gatseau Beach — The Oléron Lagoon

Located in Saint-Trojan-les-Bains, Gatseau Beach is often nicknamed "the lagoon" for its crystal-clear waters and almost tropical atmosphere. Protected by the forest, this beach offers an idyllic setting with its fine sand and shallow waters, perfect for young children. At low tide, natural pools form and allow the little ones to splash around safely. An absolutely magical spot.

5. Rémigeasse Beach — The Must-Visit

The ultimate family beach, Rémigeasse is one of the most popular among vacationers. With its golden sand, gentle waves, and summer lifeguard supervision, it ticks all the boxes for a successful family day. Beach clubs offer water activities for children, and the snack bar lets you enjoy lunch with your feet in the sand.

6. Perroche Beach — Wild and Preserved

For a wilder experience, head to Perroche Beach on the west coast of the island. Less crowded, it charms with its authenticity and magnificent sunsets. It's the ideal spot for shore fishing at low tide or building sandcastles. However, be careful of the currents — swimming is more challenging here, so it's best to stay near the shore with children.

Good to know: Perroche Beach is also renowned for its surf spots. If your teens want to try it out, this is the perfect place.

Culture & Heritage

7. Château-d'Oléron Citadel — A Journey Through Time

This impressive 17th-century fortification, built according to Vauban's plans, is a must-see on the island. Visiting the citadel is a real journey through time that fascinates both young and old. The ramparts offer stunning views of the Pertuis d'Antioche and the island bridge. The village of Château-d'Oléron, with its colorful artisan and artist cabins, is also worth a stroll.

8. Saint-Trojan-les-Bains Market — Colors and Flavors

Every morning in summer, the Saint-Trojan-les-Bains market comes alive with colors, aromas, and good cheer. It's the perfect opportunity to discover local products: seafood, goat cheese, island melons, and of course the famous Marennes-Oléron oysters. Children love strolling between the stalls and tasting specialties. An authentic experience not to be missed.

CalmRio tip: arrive early in the morning to enjoy the atmosphere and the best selection. The market takes place under the covered halls and in the surrounding streets.

Gastronomy & Local Flavors

9. Oyster Tasting — An Absolute Must

You can't visit Île d'Oléron without tasting the famous Marennes-Oléron oysters, the only ones in France to hold a Protected Geographical Indication (PGI, French: IGP). Head to an oyster shack at the port of La Cotinière or in the surrounding villages for a seaside tasting, feet in the sand. For children who aren't oyster fans, the shacks usually offer alternatives like shrimp or whelks.

10. La Cotinière Fish Market — Straight from the Boat

La Cotinière is the leading fishing port of Charente-Maritime. Every day, when the boats return, the fish market offers unparalleled freshness: sole, sea bass, sea bream, langoustines... Wide-eyed, children watch the fishermen unload their day's catch. It's both a fun and educational outing, and the best way to prepare a delicious holiday meal.

Good to know: the market takes place in the late morning, after the auction returns. Check the exact times with the tourist office.

Ready for the Oléron Adventure?

Île d'Oléron is packed with activities for the whole family, and this list is just a glimpse of everything the island has to offer. Whether you love nature, beaches, culture, or gastronomy, you'll surely find your happiness on the "Luminous Island."

The best advice we can give you? Take your time. Stroll, explore the little roads, stop in a village for a coffee on a terrace, let yourself be carried by the gentle rhythm of the island. That's the Oléron spirit.
